Jumping spiders are visual, arboreal hunters. Almost every enclosure mistake comes from
treating them like a terrestrial spider that happens to climb.

## Size and orientation

**Vertical beats horizontal.** Jumping spiders spend their time up high and build their
retreats near the top of whatever space they have.

| Stage | Rough minimum |
| --- | --- |
| Spiderling (i1–i3) | 5 × 5 × 8 cm |
| Juvenile (i4–i6) | 10 × 10 × 15 cm |
| Adult (small species) | 15 × 15 × 25 cm |
| Adult (*Phidippus*) | 20 × 20 × 30 cm |

Bigger is fine as long as prey can still be found. A spiderling in a large enclosure may
genuinely fail to encounter its food.

## Ventilation

This is the single most important variable and the one most often got wrong.

Jumping spiders need **cross-ventilation** — airflow moving through the enclosure, not a single
patch of mesh on the lid. Two vented panels on opposite sides, or vented sides plus a vented
top, produce moving air. Stagnant humid air causes far more captive deaths than dry air does,
usually via mould and fungal problems.

If condensation persists on the walls for more than an hour after misting, there is not enough
airflow.

## Substrate

A few centimeters of coconut fibre, topsoil, or a coco/sphagnum mix. Its job is to hold a
little moisture and buffer humidity, not to be burrowed in. Keep it lightly damp at the bottom
and dry at the surface.

## Furnishing

- **Anchor points near the top.** Cork bark, twigs, silk plants — anywhere to attach a retreat.
  A spider with nowhere good to build will web into a corner, and corners are awkward to work
  around when you need to open the enclosure.
- **Open flight paths.** Leave clear space to jump across. A densely packed enclosure prevents
  the hunting behavior that makes them worth watching.
- **Nothing sharp or absorbent-and-mouldy.** Avoid untreated sponges and anything that stays
  soaked.

## Water

A light mist on one wall every day or two is enough for most species — they drink droplets
rather than from a dish. Mist one side only, so the spider always has a dry area, and never
mist directly onto the spider or its retreat.

Standing water dishes are a drowning risk for small spiders. If you use one, fill it with
pebbles or moss so there is no open surface.

## Opening it safely

Open slowly, over a table rather than over carpet, and check where the spider is first. Most
escapes happen because the lid came off while the spider was sitting on it.
