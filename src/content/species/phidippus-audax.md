The jumping spider most North Americans have already met — on a fence post, a car door, or a
windowsill. Smaller than a regal but just as bold, and often the first spider a keeper finds
themselves rather than buys.

## Appearance

Black with a white or orange triangular marking on the upper abdomen and two smaller spots
below it. Juveniles usually have orange markings that fade toward white with each molt, though
plenty of adults keep some color. The chelicerae are a strong iridescent green.

## Temperament

Bold and inquisitive, much like *P. regius*, but noticeably faster and more inclined to jump.
Their smaller size means an escape is harder to recover, so open the enclosure over a
contained surface.

## Keeping notes

- **Slightly cooler tolerant.** Their natural range extends well north, so ordinary room
  temperature is fine without supplemental heat.
- **A good candidate for a found spider.** Wild-caught adults adapt readily. If you collect one
  locally, releasing it back where you found it stays an option — worth considering, since a
  wild-caught adult female may already be gravid.
- **Enclosure of at least 15 × 15 × 25 cm** for an adult, vertical, with cross-ventilation.

## Feeding

Adults take a medium prey item every 3–4 days. House flies, blue bottles, and small crickets
all work. Because they are smaller than regals, prey size matters more — aim for something no
larger than the spider's own abdomen.
