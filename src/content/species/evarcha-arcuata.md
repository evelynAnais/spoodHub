A small, widespread Eurasian jumper that most keepers will never have heard of — and the species
behind one of the most striking pieces of jumping spider research of the last decade.

## Why it appears on this site

In 2022 Rößler and colleagues filmed *Evarcha arcuata* spiderlings overnight and documented
periodic retinal movements paired with leg curling during rest — the first evidence of an REM
sleep-like state in a terrestrial invertebrate. See [sleep and rest](/care/sleep-and-rest) for
what that found and what it does and does not mean.

They used this species for a practical reason: newly emerged spiderlings have a temporarily
translucent exoskeleton, so the retinal tubes can be filmed directly. In a pigmented adult they
are invisible.

## Appearance

Males are black-brown with bright hairs across the median and lateral areas, and the abdomen
carries dark median and transverse stripes. Females are larger and broader. As with most
salticids, the definitive identification is anatomical rather than visual — the pedipalp
structure in males and the epigyne shape in females.

At 5–7 mm this is a small spider, closer to a zebra jumper than to a regal.

## Habitat

Open habitats on low vegetation — mainly moist ground, though it also turns up on dry heathland.
That breadth of range, from Portugal to Japan, means "typical conditions" vary enormously across
its distribution.

## Keeping notes

**Captive husbandry for this species is undocumented** — which is why this page carries no
temperature or humidity range. Nobody keeps it commercially and there is no established care
sheet to draw on.

What its natural history does suggest:

- **It is small.** Prey needs to be sized accordingly — *Drosophila melanogaster* rather than
  anything larger. Enclosure mesh has to be fine enough to hold a 5 mm spider.
- **It is temperate, not tropical.** Ordinary unheated room temperature is likely closer to right
  than a warmed enclosure.
- **Moist vegetation is its usual habitat**, which suggests it tolerates more humidity than a
  *Phidippus* — but ventilation still matters more than dampness.

If you keep this species and have real numbers, this page needs them more than most.
