A tiny black-and-white striped jumper found on sunny walls, fences, and rocks across the
northern hemisphere. Charming and very easy to find, but the small size makes them a step up in
difficulty rather than a beginner species.

## Appearance

Unmistakable: black with white transverse stripes across the abdomen, giving the zebra name.
Males have conspicuously large, forward-projecting chelicerae used in displays against other
males.

## Temperament

Individually variable. Some are calm and will walk onto a hand; others are quick and prone to
bolting. At 5–9 mm an escaped zebra jumper is realistically gone, so handle over a contained
space or not at all.

## Why intermediate rather than beginner

Nothing about their care is difficult — the challenge is scale.

- **Prey must be small.** *Drosophila melanogaster* (the small flightless fruit fly) rather than
  the larger *hydei*. Anything bigger is often refused or dangerous.
- **Enclosure gaps.** Ventilation holes that safely contain a regal will not contain a zebra
  jumper. Fine mesh is essential.
- **Harder to read.** Pre-molt signs that are obvious on a 20 mm spider are subtle on a 7 mm one,
  so logged feeding refusals carry more of the weight in judging where they are in a cycle.

## Keeping notes

They are a temperate species and do not want to be kept warm. Ordinary unheated room temperature
suits them, and they tolerate the cooler end better than *Phidippus* species do. A drier
enclosure with a small water source is closer to their natural habitat than a humid one.
